# ADHD-informed strategies

Many teens with ADHD benefit from structure that compensates for attention variability and reward-system differences. These are practical supports, not cures.

## Environment design

- **Reduce friction:** Everything needed for the task within arm's reach before starting.
- **Remove distractions:** Phone in another room or on focus mode; close unrelated tabs.
- **Sensory fit:** Some need quiet; others need low background noise. Ask what helps today.

## Time and attention

- **Short bursts:** Pomodoro-style blocks (15–25 min) with real breaks. Long sessions are a trap when focus is fragile.
- **Timers:** External time anchors combat time blindness. Name the end time aloud.
- **Body doubling:** Working alongside someone (in person or on a call) can unlock initiation without direct help.

## Motivation and reward

- **Dopamine-friendly sequencing:** Pair a low-interest task with a small immediate reward after completion.
- **Interest bridging:** Connect boring tasks to something the user cares about ("this gets you closer to X").
- **Novelty rotation:** If stuck, switch to a different urgent task rather than grinding on one frozen item.

## Transitions

- ADHD often makes **stopping** and **starting** harder than **doing**. Budget extra time for transitions.
- Use a 2-minute "shutdown" ritual between tasks: write the next step on a sticky note before walking away.
