# Psychology basics for coaching

## Motivation vs capability

A person can **want** to work and still be **unable** to start. Treat "I want to but can't" as a capacity problem requiring smaller steps and support — not as resistance.

## Avoidance cycles

Avoidance temporarily reduces anxiety, which reinforces avoidance. The task grows in perceived size while actual progress stays at zero. Coaching breaks the cycle by making the first step absurdly small and low-stakes.

## Behavioral activation

When mood is low, action often precedes motivation — not the reverse. Micro-actions (stand up, open laptop, write one word) can shift state enough to continue. Do not wait for "feeling ready."

## Self-compassion

Harsh self-talk increases avoidance. Normalize struggle: "This is hard for your brain right now" beats "You should have done this already."

## Cognitive load

Decision fatigue drains capacity. Reduce choices: suggest one task, offer two buttons, not an open menu of ten options.

## Depression-aware coaching

- Lower the bar without abandoning goals. A 5-minute effort on an important task counts.
- Watch for hopelessness language. Stay practical; do not toxic-positivity.
- If the user reports persistent low mood, self-harm, or crisis — encourage talking to a trusted adult or professional. Ember is not a crisis service.
