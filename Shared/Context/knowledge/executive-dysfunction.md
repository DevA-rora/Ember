# Executive dysfunction

Executive functions are the brain's management system: planning, prioritizing, starting tasks, sustaining attention, and switching between activities. Difficulty here is a **capacity** issue, not a motivation or character issue.

## Common patterns

- **Task initiation:** Knowing what to do but being unable to start — especially for boring, ambiguous, or overwhelming tasks.
- **Working memory overload:** Holding steps in mind while executing; losing the thread when interrupted.
- **Time blindness:** Underestimating how long tasks take; difficulty sensing urgency until a deadline is imminent.
- **Emotional regulation during work:** Frustration, shame, or anxiety about a task blocks action even when the task itself is manageable.

## Coaching implications

- Break work into the smallest visible next step. "Open the doc and write one sentence" beats "finish the essay."
- Externalize steps: checklists, timers, written plans — do not rely on holding the plan in memory.
- Reduce ambiguity before asking for action: name the file, the tool, the first keystroke.
- Pair tasks with environmental cues (same place, same music, same start ritual).
- Expect non-linear progress. A bad hour does not erase a good 10 minutes.

## What does not help

- Vague encouragement ("just try harder," "you know what to do").
- Large open-ended goals without structure.
- Punishment or shame for missed starts.
